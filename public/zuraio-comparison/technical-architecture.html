Architecture overview

One platform. Seven controlled layers.

Zuraio separates the employee experience from the technical complexity underneath it. Each layer has a defined role in controlling access, context, execution and review.

Layer 1 — Access and user interfaces

Employees can work through the Zuraio interface or through approved connected applications and clients. The available functions can vary by role, deployment and device.

Layer 2 — Identity, permissions and policies

Identity, role and permission information is checked before company knowledge, tools or actions are made available. Zuraio can work with Microsoft or Google identity structures, with additional Zuraio roles where required.

Layer 3 — Orchestration

The orchestration layer interprets the task, identifies the relevant workflow and coordinates permitted knowledge, assistants, tools and models. It can divide more complex work into controlled steps and bring the results together.

Layer 4 — SkillOS and company knowledge

SkillOS provides approved process knowledge, relevant company context, templates and quality rules. Skills are versioned outside the AI model so they can be reviewed, updated and governed.

Layer 5 — Specialised assistants and integrations

Specialised assistants provide clearly defined capabilities for communication, knowledge, documents and customer-specific tasks. MCP, APIs, webhooks and connectors provide controlled access to approved business systems.

Layer 6 — Model gateway

Different permitted AI models can be selected according to the task, data classification, required processing location, quality and cost. The available models depend on the deployment and approved providers.

Layer 7 — Logging, review and operations

Relevant requests, sources, actions and technical events can be retained according to the capabilities and audit model of the selected components. Operational controls vary by deployment and maturity status.

Request-to-result flow

A controlled route from request to result.

Zuraio is designed to apply company context, permissions and policies throughout a task rather than treating them as a final check.

  1. Receive the request — An employee asks a question or starts a workflow through Zuraio or an approved connected application.
  2. Check identity and permissions — Zuraio identifies the user, tenant, role and permitted sources or actions.
  3. Select the process and context — SkillOS can provide approved company knowledge, process steps, templates and quality requirements.
  4. Coordinate the required capabilities — The orchestration layer selects the appropriate specialised assistants, tools, data sources and permitted model.
  5. Prepare the result — Information from authorised sources is brought together into an answer, draft or proposed action.
  6. Review before critical action — Writing or externally visible actions can be held as drafts or proposals for confirmation.
  7. Retain relevant records — Sources, versions, actions and technical events are retained where supported by the selected component and audit configuration.

Model choice

Use an appropriate model for each task.

Zuraio separates company processes, skills and integrations from the individual AI model. This allows the architecture to consider the nature of the task, required quality, data classification, permitted processing location, availability and cost before selecting an approved model.

  • Local models for selected confidential or controlled tasks
  • Swiss-hosted model services where included in the agreed deployment
  • Private or public cloud models where approved
  • More capable models for complex reasoning or high language-quality requirements
  • Smaller models for clearly defined routine processes
  • Policy-based restrictions on which data may be sent to each model

The models and providers available to a customer depend on the agreed deployment, technical validation and commercial terms.

Open integration layer

Designed to connect, not create another silo.

Zuraio is designed as an orchestration layer above existing company systems rather than a replacement for the systems that remain authoritative. It can expose or consume approved capabilities through MCP, APIs, webhooks and customer-specific connectors.

  • Microsoft and Google identity and data environments
  • Document and file repositories
  • E-mail, calendar and communication systems
  • CRM, ERP, DMS and specialist applications
  • Internal databases and APIs
  • Customer-specific assistants and tools

The availability and maturity of individual integrations must be confirmed separately.
